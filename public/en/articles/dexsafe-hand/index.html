LEAP hand reaches 95.2% of in-hand pose targets with fewer drops

A dexterous hand can reach the right pose and still drop its object. Researchers using the LEAP hand treat that risk as a separate safety problem. They train in simulation without human demonstrations and add an adjustable residual filter to moderate risky actions.

Across 13.3 hours of real-hand testing, four objects and more than 4,800 unseen goals, the base policy reached 95.2% of target poses within 2 cm and 0.4 radians. The filter cut drops by 75% while retaining 91.5% target success. The trade-off is measurable, but performance on other hands and industrial duty cycles remains unproven.

About the newspaper

The Replicant Truth is a digest from the world of physical artificial intelligence, created almost entirely with the help of AI.

This project is a tribute to Roy Batty from Blade Runner. To its author, Batty embodies a question that grows more urgent in our digital present: what makes us human — and how do we hold on to our humanity amid an avalanche of technological change?

Roy Batty and his famous monologue mean a great deal to me, as they do to many people. I claim no rights to the character, his screen portrayal or the monologue.
