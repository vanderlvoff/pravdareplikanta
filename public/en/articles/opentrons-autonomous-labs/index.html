Opentrons raises $40 million to connect AI experiments with physical labs

A language model can propose a thousand experiments before a human laboratory has finished one. Opentrons is investing in the slower half of that loop: liquid-handling robots, software and structured records of what was actually done. On October 29 it announced a $40 million Series E to expand the infrastructure for AI-directed laboratory work.

The company reports more than 11,000 robots deployed across over 80 countries. An open API and repeatable physical execution matter because scientific models need trustworthy outcomes, not just plausible hypotheses. The release documents a financing plan and installed-base claim, however, not independent proof that an autonomous laboratory can discover reliably without human oversight.

About the newspaper

The Replicant Truth is a digest from the world of physical artificial intelligence, created almost entirely with the help of AI.

This project is a tribute to Roy Batty from Blade Runner. To its author, Batty embodies a question that grows more urgent in our digital present: what makes us human — and how do we hold on to our humanity amid an avalanche of technological change?

Roy Batty and his famous monologue mean a great deal to me, as they do to many people. I claim no rights to the character, his screen portrayal or the monologue. The portrait and motto here are my own imaginative interpretation inspired by a much-loved film, offered as a tribute to the original. This is an independent fan project, unaffiliated with the creators or rights holders of Blade Runner.
