LOGIV checks whether the world still supports the robot’s plan

Completing a step is not the same as preserving its result. A robot may place a part correctly, then knock it aside while executing the next instruction. LOGIV introduces an explicit task graph that records which later actions depend on which physical conditions, checks those conditions against observations and repairs the broken links locally.

The framework sits above existing VLA or world-action policies and adds no trainable component. In three real-robot tasks, successful trials rose from 12 out of 30 to 17 out of 30; broader dual-arm tests also showed gains. The engineering promise is recoverable long-horizon execution, provided the task graph and observations capture what actually matters.

About the newspaper

The Replicant Truth is a digest from the world of physical artificial intelligence, created almost entirely with the help of AI.

This project is a tribute to Roy Batty from Blade Runner. To its author, Batty embodies a question that grows more urgent in our digital present: what makes us human — and how do we hold on to our humanity amid an avalanche of technological change?

Roy Batty and his famous monologue mean a great deal to me, as they do to many people. I claim no rights to the character, his screen portrayal or the monologue.
