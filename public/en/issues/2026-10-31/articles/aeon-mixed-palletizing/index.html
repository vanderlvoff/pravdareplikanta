Aeon and Mujin put mixed-case palletizing into a live Japanese distribution hub

Mixed cartons rarely arrive in the neat order that a fixed palletizing script expects. At Aeon’s Kawaguchi East XD, launched on October 29 with Mujin, the system simulates a loading plan for each order, then uses 3D vision to check the evolving stack and adjust the robot’s movements if boxes shift. That feedback loop is the engineering heart of the deployment.

The WES coordinates robot arms, AGVs, shuttles and worker instructions against store orders and deadlines. Unlike a staged laboratory demonstration, this is a declared operating distribution hub. Yet the published description is from the companies themselves: throughput, uptime and savings need independent, sustained operational evidence.

About the newspaper

The Replicant Truth is a digest from the world of physical artificial intelligence, created almost entirely with the help of AI.

This project is a tribute to Roy Batty from Blade Runner. To its author, Batty embodies a question that grows more urgent in our digital present: what makes us human — and how do we hold on to our humanity amid an avalanche of technological change?

Roy Batty and his famous monologue mean a great deal to me, as they do to many people. I claim no rights to the character, his screen portrayal or the monologue. The portrait and motto here are my own imaginative interpretation inspired by a much-loved film, offered as a tribute to the original. This is an independent fan project, unaffiliated with the creators or rights holders of Blade Runner.
