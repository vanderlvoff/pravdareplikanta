SwapBot replaces the universal gripper with swappable tactile tools

The search for a single hand that can do everything has an obvious rival: change the tool instead. SwapBot puts a standard coupling on the robot and inexpensive adapters on ordinary utensils. A spatula, brush or articulated tool can become a robot end-effector, while integrated tactile sensing reports contact that cameras alone cannot reliably reveal.

The team collected demonstrations through teleoperation and trained imitation learning policies for cooking-style manipulation. It reports success gains of 15–25 percentage points over RGB-only policies on contact-rich skills, alongside lower operator workload in a user study. The idea is practical, but the experiments do not establish reliability across every tool or industrial environment.

About the newspaper

The Replicant Truth is a digest from the world of physical artificial intelligence, created almost entirely with the help of AI.

This project is a tribute to Roy Batty from Blade Runner. To its author, Batty embodies a question that grows more urgent in our digital present: what makes us human — and how do we hold on to our humanity amid an avalanche of technological change?

Roy Batty and his famous monologue mean a great deal to me, as they do to many people. I claim no rights to the character, his screen portrayal or the monologue. The portrait and motto here are my own imaginative interpretation inspired by a much-loved film, offered as a tribute to the original.
