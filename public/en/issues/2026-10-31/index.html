Replicant Pravda — Physical AI, 31 October 2026

Eight developments in embodied AI: tactile tools, simulation, recoverable task execution, Japanese warehouse deployment and the economics of scaling.

News

SwapBot replaces the universal gripper with swappable tactile tools

The search for a single hand that can do everything has an obvious rival: change the tool instead. SwapBot puts a standard coupling on the robot and inexpensive adapters on ordinary utensils. A spatula, brush or articulated tool can become a robot end-effector, while integrated tactile sensing reports contact that cameras alone cannot reliably reveal.

Gym-Anything lets coding agents build the robot training worlds

A robot policy can look capable in a familiar simulator and still fail when the world changes. Gym-Anything attacks the supply side of that problem: coding agents create and audit new physical simulations, rather than waiting for experts to hand-build each environment. The resulting RoboWorld benchmark spans 25 simulations and 25 test tasks.

Read more… →

TouchBridge carries human fingertip contact into robot training

When a person turns an object in the hand, the important information is not just where the fingers went. It is where they touched and how the contact changed. TouchBridge combines a wearable demonstration interface with tactile-conditioned retargeting, preserving fingertip contact relations as examples are converted for a robot hand.

Read more… →

LOGIV checks whether the world still supports the robot’s plan

Completing a step is not the same as preserving its result. A robot may place a part correctly, then knock it aside while executing the next instruction. LOGIV introduces an explicit task graph that records which later actions depend on which physical conditions, checks those conditions against observations and repairs the broken links locally.

Read more… →

Aeon and Mujin put mixed-case palletizing into a live Japanese distribution hub

Mixed cartons rarely arrive in the neat order that a fixed palletizing script expects. At Aeon’s Kawaguchi East XD, launched on October 29 with Mujin, the system simulates a loading plan for each order, then uses 3D vision to check the evolving stack and adjust the robot’s movements if boxes shift. That feedback loop is the engineering heart of the deployment.

Read more… →

Agility updates the capital available for scaling Digit 5

The distance from a convincing humanoid demonstration to a serviceable fleet is measured partly in capital. Agility Robotics and Churchill XI now expect roughly $465 million in gross proceeds from their proposed combination, according to a filing dated October 30. The companies target closing on November 4 and Nasdaq trading on November 5, subject to approval and other conditions.

Read more… →

Opentrons raises $40 million to connect AI experiments with physical labs

A language model can propose a thousand experiments before a human laboratory has finished one. Opentrons is investing in the slower half of that loop: liquid-handling robots, software and structured records of what was actually done. On October 29 it announced a $40 million Series E to expand the infrastructure for AI-directed laboratory work.

Read more… →

Bleu Robotics bets on few-shot teaching rather than general-purpose hype

Industrial robotics has a stubborn problem: the next product variant can invalidate yesterday’s carefully programmed cell. Bleu Robotics, an Inria spinout, has raised €5 million to pursue a narrower alternative to universal robot intelligence. Its few-shot imitation learning approach tries to infer a workstation task from roughly ten operator demonstrations, using existing perception and motion-control tools.

Issue glossary

Terms and definitions from the editorial dataset.

VLA

Vision-Language-Action model: turns visual observations and instructions into robot actions.

Tactile sensing

Measuring physical contact or force through sensors on a tool or hand.

Imitation learning

Learning a robot policy from demonstrated actions.

End-effector

A robot’s tool or gripper attached at the end of its arm.

Sim-to-real

Transfer of policies or insights from simulation to physical hardware.

Task graph

Representation of actions and the conditions on which later steps depend.

WES

Warehouse Execution System: coordinates equipment and operational work.

3D vision

Visual sensing that estimates object geometry and spatial placement.

Palletizing

Arranging boxes or goods into stable loads for transport.

Few-shot

Adapting to a new task from a small number of examples.
