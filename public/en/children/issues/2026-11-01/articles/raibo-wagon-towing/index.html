How a four-legged robot learns to pull a wagon around obstacles

Pulling a suitcase around a corner can be harder than walking around the corner yourself. The wheels may catch an obstacle even when you pass safely. A four-legged robot pulling a wagon faces a similar problem. Researchers built a navigation system that considers the path of both the robot and its trailer.

The wagon changes how sharply the robot can turn and how much space it needs. The planning program must predict where the load will travel after each movement. That is different from finding an empty path for a robot traveling alone. The connection between the robot and the wagon also matters.

In tests with a real quadruped, the researchers used routes[1] 15 meters long. The system completed 85 percent of 40 trials and handled loads of up to 40 kilograms. Those figures describe the tested setup, not every possible wagon, surface or route. Some attempts[2] still failed.

Before a system like this works reliably in warehouses or public places, it needs tests with longer journeys, uneven ground, moving people and wheels that become stuck. The lesson is that the robot must plan for everything it is pulling, not just its own feet. The robot in this research was called RAIBO.
