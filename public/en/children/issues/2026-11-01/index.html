Why changing tools can help a robot handle different jobs

Imagine stirring a salad, turning food in a pan and cutting an ingredient. One tool would be awkward for all three jobs. Researchers designed a robot interface that changes ordinary tools without replacing the whole arm. The work appeared on October 29 and describes a laboratory system rather than a ready-made kitchen assistant.

Read the article →

How a four-legged robot learns to pull a wagon around obstacles

Pulling a suitcase around a corner can be harder than walking around the corner yourself. The wheels may catch an obstacle even when you pass safely. A four-legged robot pulling a wagon faces a similar problem. Researchers built a navigation system that considers the path of both the robot and its trailer.

Read the article →

How can we compare robots that demonstrate different tricks?

One robot moves blocks quickly while another carefully lifts a delicate object. Which is better? A single success number cannot answer that when the tasks and testing conditions differ. Researchers proposed a framework that asks readers to look more closely at what each experiment actually measured.

Read the article →

Why a soft robot gripper should tolerate small mistakes

When you pick up a cup, your fingers adjust a little to its shape. A robot benefits from similar flexibility, especially if its camera estimates the object's position imperfectly. Researchers proposed designing soft grippers for these small errors rather than assuming that every approach will be perfectly aligned.

Read the article →

A five-finger robot tries packing an online order

Packing an online order means choosing the correct products, placing them in a box and avoiding damage. At an event in Tokyo, a company demonstrated a robot with a five-finger hand doing similar work. The event took place on October 27 and 28, while the company reported it on October 30.

About the newspaper

The Replicant Truth is a digest from the world of physical artificial intelligence, created almost entirely with the help of AI.

This project is a tribute to Roy Batty from Blade Runner. To its author, Batty embodies a question that grows more urgent in our digital present: what makes us human — and how do we hold on to our humanity amid an avalanche of technological change?
